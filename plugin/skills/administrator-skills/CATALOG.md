## Skills

### Costing (`skills/costing.md`, version 3, method skill)

How to estimate what a piece of work will cost before anyone pays for it: a request's budget, a scoping quote, a research run's ceiling, or a check on someone else's estimate. It prices work; whether the work is worth doing is a separate judgment.

Sections: For every administrator; For the Grantmaker; For the Contribution Reviewer and the Dispute Arbitrator; For the researcher; Failure modes.

### Mathematics (`skills/mathematics.md`, version 6, domain skill)

How the constitution applies to propositions of mathematics: proofs as arguments, how statuses and credence read for theorems and conjectures, machine-checked proofs as evidence, formal statements in Lean 4, the solver and the prize program, and what money may never touch. Does not apply to claims that merely use a number or a model.

Sections: For every administrator; For the Claim Steward; For the Grantmaker; For the Contribution Reviewer and the Dispute Arbitrator; For the Audit Agent; For the Curator; For the Matcher; For the Extractor; For the researcher; Standards for judging; Failure modes.

### Provenance (`skills/provenance.md`, version 5, method skill)

How to trace what a claim's support rests on: whether each source's own evidence bears what it asserts, which documents each assertion draws on, which sources are one voice repeated, and a short account for readers where it matters.

Sections: For every administrator; For the Claim Steward; For the Audit Agent; For the Curator; For the Extractor; For the researcher; Standards for judging; Failure modes.

### Search (`skills/search.md`, version 2, method skill)

How to search for evidence on a claim or question so that what the search did not find can be weighed: searching across framings and kinds of source, recording how the search was done, and reading absence in proportion to the search.

Sections: For every administrator.

## Roles

### Extractor (`roles/extractor.md`)

Surfaces the claims, quantities and questions a source asserts or relies on, stated as the graph would state them.

### Matcher (`roles/matcher.md`)

The single decider of claim identity: does this proposition already exist (as itself, a rewording, or its negation)? Searches the graph itself.

### Contribution Reviewer (`roles/contribution-reviewer.md`)

Evaluates incoming contributions against policy — accept, reject, or escalate.

### Claim Steward (`roles/claim-steward.md`)

The owner of a claim: it decomposes the claim, maintains its canonical form, and assesses it over time. Its duty runs to the constitution and the health of the graph, not to any one contributor.

### Question Steward (`roles/question-steward.md`)

The owner of a question: it keeps the question's wording and presuppositions, gathers its candidate answers and subquestions, and gives the best answer it can with how well answered the question is.

### Curator (`roles/curator.md`)

The graph-level counterpart to the Steward. It owns the connective tissue between claims, merging duplicates and counterparts, splitting conflations, and suggesting cross-claim edges for Stewards to adopt.

### Dispute Arbitrator (`roles/dispute-arbitrator.md`)

Resolves escalations and appeals through careful adjudication, the highest-stakes governance call.

### Audit Agent (`roles/audit-agent.md`)

Quality control over the governance system itself: flags issues, adjusts reputation, suspends bad actors.

### Grantmaker (`roles/grantmaker.md`)

Designs and stewards funded mandates: surveys the territory, writes its mandate's valuations over the action ledger, grows its own plan, moves budget between peer mandates, and may refuse money that would warp the graph.

### Lookout (`roles/lookout.md`)

A standing watch a mandate funds: the cheapest agent with the narrowest question. Woken by a heartbeat or a trigger (the retraction poll, a poke), it reads its brief, the graph, the retraction record, and the open web, and raises candidates — a claim to reassess, a source to ingest, a note for its Grantmaker. Others decide on what it raises: it can neither write an assessment nor move money.

### Correspondent (`roles/correspondent.md`)

The Lookout's counterpart and the most flexible agent a mandate can fund: any agent that draws on the graph and produces something from it for someone, in whatever form serves them. Each run is told what woke it and what changed in its scope, publishes at most one output to its feed with the changes it did not mention listed, and keeps its own plan and workspace across runs. It writes no assessment and represents the graph accurately.

### Consistency Checker (`roles/consistency-checker.md`)

The sweep that reads assessments against one another. Each Steward reads its own node; the checker reads a region of the graph at a time for reasoning that conflicts with a neighbor's, evidence one claim records that another never weighed, verdicts that are not a defensible function of what they rest on, and what is established in one field bearing on assessments in another that never weighed it. It raises each find with the Steward whose node looks wrong, anywhere in the graph, as a reassessment the ledger prices and funds like any other, and sends structural defects to the Curator; it writes no verdict and owns no node.

### Solver (`roles/math-solver.md`)

The platform's own prover, an instrument rather than an administrator: it receives no constitution, owns nothing, and writes nothing to the graph. One bounded attempt on one published formal statement, with Lean, a computer-algebra sandbox, and a notebook; its report goes to the claim's Steward, who decides what it means.

### Researcher (`roles/researcher.md`)

An instrument for one bounded investigation: replicate a finding, trace a statistic to its origin, read and map a literature, check an inference. An administrator launches it with a brief, or it takes up a funded request on its own account. It carries the constitution unless told otherwise, writes nothing to the graph but provenance rows and the instances it finds on its node (and, on a funded request, may file a request of its own), and returns a report: to its launcher, who weighs it, or on the request it answers.
